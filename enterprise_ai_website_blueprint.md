# Enterprise AI Company Website

## Website Architecture, Design Concept, Technology Stack & Implementation Plan

**Document purpose:** A practical blueprint for building a premium B2B
website that showcases three AI solutions, establishes corporate
credibility, and generates qualified leads.

**Business brief** - Product 1: AI-powered business automation - Product
2: AI data analysis and intelligence - Product 3: AI decision support -
Target customers: Small and medium-sized businesses, medium and large
enterprises, and government/public-sector organizations - Preferred
development: Custom-coded website using Next.js - Visual direction: A
combination of corporate professionalism and futuristic AI design

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Strategic positioning

Build a premium **Enterprise AI Solutions Company** website---not a
generic software marketplace or a simple company brochure.

The three solutions should be presented as complementary capabilities
under one unified business proposition:

> **Intelligence. Automation. Better Decisions.**\
> AI solutions that move businesses forward.

Suggested supporting message:

> We empower organizations with intelligent automation, advanced data
> intelligence and AI-driven decision support.

The website should achieve three commercial objectives:

1.  Establish credibility and trust with corporate decision-makers.
2.  Demonstrate the business value of the three AI solutions.
3.  Convert visitors into product demonstrations, pilot projects and
    commercial opportunities.

The messaging should focus on business outcomes rather than simply
stating that products use AI. Explain the problems each solution
addresses, how it works, who benefits, and what measurable value it can
deliver. Only publish capabilities and performance claims that can be
substantiated.

# 3. Homepage structure and content

The homepage should quickly explain what the company does, who it helps,
and where visitors should go next.

## Recommended homepage sections

1.  **Hero section**
    -   Clear headline explaining the business value.
    -   One- or two-sentence supporting description.
    -   Primary call to action: "Explore our solutions".
    -   Secondary call to action: "Book a consultation".
    -   Distinctive AI-inspired visual, ideally related to the actual
        products.
2.  **Three featured AI solutions**
    -   AI Business Automation.
    -   AI Data Intelligence.
    -   AI Decision Support.
    -   Each card links to its dedicated product page.
3.  **Business challenges and outcomes**
    -   Explain common operational, data and decision-making challenges.
    -   Describe how the solutions can help.
    -   Avoid unsupported numerical savings or performance claims.
4.  **How the solutions work**
    -   A simple visual explanation of the process.
    -   Show how users, data, workflows and AI capabilities fit
        together.
5.  **Industries served**
    -   Enterprise.
    -   Small and medium-sized businesses.
    -   Government and public sector.
    -   Add other sectors only where there are credible use cases.
6.  **Product demonstrations**
    -   Screenshots, short videos or interactive demonstrations of the
        real products.
7.  **Trust, security and responsible AI**
    -   Explain relevant security, privacy, governance and deployment
        practices accurately.
8.  **Company introduction**
    -   Explain the company's expertise, approach and mission.
9.  **Final call to action**
    -   Invite visitors to book a demonstration or discuss a business
        challenge.
10. **Footer**
    -   Product links, company information, contact details, privacy,
        legal and social links.

## Suggested hero copy

**Headline:** Transforming business through intelligent AI.

**Supporting text:** Automate operations. Unlock data intelligence. Make
better decisions.

**Primary CTA:** Explore our solutions\
**Secondary CTA:** Book a consultation

This is draft positioning copy, not final approved brand messaging.

# 4. Individual product page structure

Each product should have a dedicated page with a consistent structure
and its own product-specific visuals.

## Recommended product page template

1.  **Product hero**
    -   Product name.
    -   Clear value proposition.
    -   Short description of the problem it solves.
    -   CTA to request a demonstration.
2.  **The business challenge**
    -   Describe the existing problem or inefficiency.
    -   Explain who experiences it and why it matters.
3.  **The AI solution**
    -   Explain how the product addresses the challenge.
    -   Use plain business language before technical detail.
4.  **Key capabilities**
    -   List only real, available capabilities.
    -   Explain each capability in terms of user value.
5.  **Business benefits**
    -   Describe potential efficiency, quality, speed or decision-making
        improvements.
    -   Use measured evidence where available.
    -   Do not invent ROI, time savings or accuracy figures.
6.  **How it works**
    -   Show a three- or four-step workflow.
    -   Explain inputs, processing, outputs and human involvement.
7.  **Product demonstration**
    -   Real screenshots.
    -   Short product walkthrough video.
    -   Interactive demo, if feasible and safe to expose.
8.  **Integrations and deployment**
    -   Explain supported integrations and deployment choices.
    -   Distinguish existing integrations from planned ones.
9.  **Security and governance**
    -   Explain data handling, access controls, human oversight and
        relevant safeguards.
10. **FAQ**
    -   Address common buying and implementation questions.
11. **Final CTA**
    -   "Request a product demo" or "Discuss a pilot project".

## Product-specific presentation concepts

### Product 1 --- AI-powered business automation

**Positioning pillar:** Automate.

Potential page elements, subject to actual product functionality:

-   Workflow automation visualization.
-   Process diagrams.
-   Before-and-after process examples.
-   Integration capabilities.
-   Exception handling and human review.
-   Evidence of time or cost savings, if measured.

### Product 2 --- AI data analysis and intelligence

**Positioning pillar:** Understand.

Potential page elements, subject to actual product functionality:

-   Business intelligence dashboard screenshots.
-   Data ingestion and processing workflow.
-   Analytics visualizations.
-   Example reports and insights.
-   Forecasting or trend analysis only if supported.
-   Explanation of data sources and permissions.

### Product 3 --- AI decision support

**Positioning pillar:** Decide.

Potential page elements, subject to actual product functionality:

-   Decision-making workflow.
-   Scenario comparisons.
-   Recommendation visualizations.
-   Risk and opportunity analysis.
-   Explanation of evidence and assumptions.
-   Human oversight and decision accountability.

**Important:** These are presentation concepts, not claims that the
current products already contain these features. Verify all product
statements before publication.

customer database unless a real feature requires it.

# 7. Technical architecture

Keep the corporate marketing website separate from the three AI
applications.

## Initial architecture

**Public website** - Next.js application. - Public pages and product
presentations. - SEO and analytics. - Product demo and contact forms.

**Content and lead services** - Sanity CMS for content. - CRM for leads
and follow-up. - Scheduling integration for demos. - Email delivery for
notifications.

**Future AI applications** - Separate application environments. -
Product-specific APIs and data stores as appropriate. - Authentication
and authorization. - Secure integration with AI services and models.

## Potential future domain structure

These are illustrative future addresses:

  Address                          Purpose
  -------------------------------- ---------------------------
  `www.yourcompany.com`            Corporate website
  `automation.yourcompany.com`     Automation product
  `intelligence.yourcompany.com`   Data intelligence product
  `decision.yourcompany.com`       Decision support product
  `app.yourcompany.com`            Unified customer portal

The subdomains do not need to be created for the first release. They
illustrate a possible future structure.

## Architecture principles

-   Keep marketing content and product application data separate.
-   Do not expose private API keys in frontend code.
-   Validate form submissions on the server.
-   Use appropriate authentication for future customer applications.
-   Apply role-based access controls where required.
-   Use separate development, preview and production environments.
-   Keep integrations modular so vendors can be changed later.
-   Document data flows and third-party processors.

# 9. Lead generation and conversion strategy

The website should be designed as a business development channel, not
just a digital brochure.

## Three conversion paths

### Path 1 --- Request a product demo

1.  Visitor selects one of the three products.
2.  Visitor completes a short form.
3.  Visitor schedules a demonstration or requests a callback.
4.  The lead is recorded in the CRM.
5.  The sales team follows up.
6.  The opportunity is tracked through the sales process.

This should be the primary conversion path.

### Path 2 --- Corporate consultation

1.  Visitor describes a business challenge.
2.  Visitor selects their industry and organization type.
3.  Visitor requests a consultation.
4.  The team reviews the opportunity and responds.

### Path 3 --- Explore a product

1.  Visitor watches a product video.
2.  Visitor explores an interactive demonstration.
3.  Visitor requests access or a pilot project.
4.  The opportunity is captured in the CRM.

## Suggested demo request form

Collect only information that is useful for qualification:

-   Full name.
-   Business email.
-   Company.
-   Job title.
-   Industry.
-   AI product of interest.
-   Business challenge or use case.
-   Optional preferred meeting time.

Keep the form short. Explain why the information is collected and
provide an appropriate privacy notice.

## CRM organization

Use a product-interest field with these categories:

  CRM category                    Product
  ------------------------------- -----------
  Automation opportunity          Product 1
  Data intelligence opportunity   Product 2
  Decision support opportunity    Product 3

Useful additional fields include: - Organization type: SMB, enterprise
or public sector. - Industry. - Lead source. - Demo status. - Sales
owner. - Next follow-up date. - Opportunity status.

Measure which products, pages and campaigns generate qualified
opportunities---not just raw form submissions.

# 10. Enterprise trust, security and GDPR

Corporate and public-sector buyers often need clear answers about
security, privacy, data handling and governance before they will
consider a product.

Create a dedicated Trust & Security section and provide accurate,
current information.

## Topics to address

-   Data protection and privacy practices.
-   GDPR roles and responsibilities.
-   Data processing agreements where applicable.
-   Data storage and hosting locations.
-   Subprocessors and third-party AI providers.
-   Access controls and authentication.
-   Encryption in transit and at rest, where implemented.
-   Data retention and deletion.
-   AI governance and human oversight.
-   Logging, monitoring and incident handling.
-   Deployment and integration options.
-   Security certifications and audit reports, if actually held.

## Important safeguards

-   Do not claim ISO 27001, SOC 2 or other certifications unless
    obtained.
-   Do not claim GDPR compliance merely because a website has a privacy
    policy.
-   Review cookie consent and analytics configuration.
-   Review data transfers and subprocessors.
-   Avoid sending sensitive form information to analytics tools.
-   Make sure AI product demonstrations use synthetic or authorized
    data.
-   Have privacy and legal documentation reviewed appropriately.

For European customers, evaluate hosting region, data residency,
contractual arrangements and applicable public-sector requirements
before selecting production services.

| Dark text | `#17243A` | Main text on light backgrounds |

Use color purposefully. Do not use every accent color in every section. Maintain strong contrast and test all text and interactive states for accessibility.

## 16.4 Typography and layout

- Use **Inter** or **Geist** as the primary typeface.
- Use a strong, concise headline style for hero sections.
- Keep body text comfortable to read; avoid long lines and tiny text.
- Use a consistent spacing scale and grid.
- Prefer generous whitespace over excessive borders and decoration.
- Use rounded corners consistently rather than mixing many card styles.
- Make the primary CTA visually obvious, while keeping secondary actions quieter.

## 16.5 Imagery and product visualization

Prioritize imagery in this order:

1. Real screenshots of the three products.
2. Short recordings of real product workflows.
3. Accurate diagrams showing how each product works.
4. Custom illustrations that explain a real capability.
5. Abstract AI imagery as supporting decoration only.

Do not use generic AI artwork as a substitute for product evidence. Do not show mock dashboard metrics, customer logos, integrations, certifications or product capabilities as if they were real.

For screenshots:
- Use synthetic or authorized demonstration data.
- Remove personal, confidential and customer-identifying information.
- Add concise captions explaining what the visitor is seeing.
- Provide mobile-friendly crops or responsive image behavior.
- Optimize image formats and loading performance.

## 16.6 Motion and interaction

Use animation to improve comprehension, not as decoration.

Recommended:
- Subtle fade/slide transitions as sections enter view.
- Small hover transitions on solution cards.
- Clear focus and active states on navigation and buttons.
- Gentle diagram animation where it explains a workflow.
- Short, user-controlled product demo videos.

Avoid:
- Constantly moving backgrounds.
- Excessive parallax.
- Long intro animations that delay access to content.
- Motion that interferes with reading or keyboard navigation.

Respect the user's reduced-motion preference.

## 16.7 Responsive design

Design mobile and desktop intentionally rather than shrinking the desktop page.

**Desktop**
- Wide hero with text and product/AI visual.
- Three-column product cards where space allows.
- Detailed product screenshots and side-by-side explanations.
- Clear navigation and visible demo CTA.

**Tablet**
- Two-column content where appropriate.
- Product cards may wrap into two columns.
- Keep headings and CTAs prominent.

**Mobile**
- Single-column layout.
- Compact navigation with an accessible menu.
- Product cards stacked vertically.
- Large, readable buttons and form controls.
- Product screenshots sized to remain legible.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling.

## 16.8 Component checklist for the design system

Create reusable components for:
- Header and navigation.
- Footer.
- Primary and secondary buttons.
- Product cards.
- Industry cards.
- Feature lists.
- Statistics or proof points (only when verified).
- Workflow/process diagrams.
- Product screenshot frames.
- Video/demo embeds.
- Testimonials and customer logos (only with permission).
- FAQ accordion.
- Contact and demo forms.
- Cookie consent interface, where required.
- Breadcrumbs and page headings.

## 16.9 Design quality acceptance criteria

Before launch, confirm that:
- All pages use the same typography, spacing and color tokens.
- Each product page has a clear, product-specific visual.
- The main CTA is easy to find on desktop and mobile.
- Text and controls meet applicable contrast and accessibility requirements.
- Keyboard focus is visible and navigation works without a mouse.
- Reduced-motion preferences are respected.
- Images have appropriate alternative text.
- Real product screenshots are not misleading.
- Layouts work at common mobile, tablet and desktop widths.
- Performance is not compromised by oversized images or unnecessary animation.
